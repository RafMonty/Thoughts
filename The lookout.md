I state across the valley and down the gorge.
The sun is setting behind me.
Like the last years of my life I walked here alone.
No one knows where I am or what I’m up to.
This is where I came to write, but those days are gone now.
I gathered my thoughts, and for many a moon shared them with my text editor. Nothing fancy. Like me, bare bones but does the job.
A breeze caresses my clammy skin, it feels nice. Refreshing, I am alone at this lookout, no one comes in the evening. I’ve been here before, on a clear night he stares are amazing.
Sunset is filling the summer dry landscape with an orange purplish hue.
I twist in the breeze, and look down the gorge, this is a moment in time I would have shared, a blanket, a cuddle maybe even a red. Magic hour they call it. Magic I haven’t felt for a lifetime. Now it has been the sting of isolation. I’ve been told I am selfish for the thinking I’ve done. That I don’t think of others. It’s getting darker now. The birds are returning home. Maybe soon I’ll be on my way. I twist back in the breeze. I feel at peace. Grief is only temporary I will soon me forgotten. Anyone “close” to me will not notice me gone before a stranger finds me. I didn’t want to cause alarm so I’ve left a note on the path letting them know what is ahead and if they have young ones not to proceed. It’s not messy, it’s peaceful and quiet. It is on my terms. The last act of control in my life. So much was at the mercy of others, my isolation, anxiety, trauma. I don’t pretend to be the victim, I don’t want to lay any blame. I just wanted it to stop. The worst most addictive drugs are hope and companionship. one is addictive, and one has the worst withdrawal of them all. It is dusk now the first stars poke through the blue blanket of sky. I stoped breathing a while ago. Now I’m just floating in the moment. Peaceful listening to the noise of the bush coming to life at night, a thought crosses my mind. I wonder if I will have time to haunt this lookout, paper in photos, make noises but no one can interact in meaningful way. That would be a sad irony indeed. But a role I am well rehoused to play. The colour is fading now, and the view is narrowing. All I can see now is darkness and a pinprick of light. No tunnel no voices calling, no one I m here in life alone, in death alone and I enter the afterlife alone. It’s not black now, I just have no vision, an absence of sight. But the rustling of the bushes and trees near the lookout. Even that is echoing and fading into the distance. Finally I had the courage to bring this to an end.
