I see her here alone but for her pooch.She must have seen me here alone but for my thoughts.We exchange smiles awkwardly across the coffee shopfrom our separate tables.
I will always pat the dog as I go past.Her dog likes me.The small talk is forced, but always consistent and unique.Today she had been to the beach.Her dog likes people for the pats and attention.
I see her alone again today. I pat the dog.My mind plays over introducing myself.The pointlessness of being alone.I rehearse all the lines, but no replies.I am foolish and a creep.What could she see in me.
I’ve been alone so long nowa polite smile from a stranger feels like affection.I am starving.In a desert I see an oasis but it is a mirage.I find a drop of water and it’s a pail.I am doomed to walk this desert of drips and miragesnot knowing right from wrong, left from right.
But I think—what if she is in her own desert,and feeling the same.All we need to do is connect, talk, possibly touch.
I look over again and pretend my attention is on her dog.I smile not from the dog,but the warmth that stranger’s smile brings me.
I finish my coffee and leave.Nothing said, nothing gained.I’ll spare her the train wreck and embarrassment that is me
