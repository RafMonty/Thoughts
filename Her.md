I see her here alone but for her pooch. 
She must have seen me here alone but for my thoughts. 
We exchange smiles awkwardly across the coffee shop from our separate tables.
I always pat the dog as I go past. 
Her dog likes me. 
The small talk is forced, but always consistent and unique. She’d been to the beach. 
Her dog likes people for the pats and attention.
I see her alone again. I pat the dog. 
My mind plays over introducing myself. 
The pointlessness of being alone. 
I rehearse all the lines, but no replies. 
I am foolish and a creep. 
What could she see in me?
I’ve been alone so long that a polite smile from a stranger feels like affection, pathetic.
I am dehydrated and lost in a desert, I see an oasis but it’s a mirage, find a drop of water and it’s a pail. 
I am doomed to walk this desert of drips and mirages not knowing right from wrong, left from right.
But what if she is in her own desert, feeling the same? All we need is to connect, talk, possibly touch.
I look over and pretend my attention is on her dog.
I smile not from the dog, but from the warmth that the stranger’s smile brings me.
I finish my coffee and leave. 
Nothing said, nothing gained. 
I’ll spare her the train wreck and embarrassment…
that is me.
